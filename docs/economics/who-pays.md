# Who pays: issuance or fees

!!! warning "Stub — not written yet"

    This page is an outline. Delete this box when you fill it in.

**[Permanent](../about/forks.md).** There are only two sources of revenue, and that's arithmetic rather than protocol rules.

Given that [security has to be paid for](security-budget.md), the next question is who pays. There are exactly two possibilities.

## The argument to make

**Issuance** creates new currency and gives it to whoever did the work. Nobody is billed, so it looks free — but the existing holders now own a smaller share of the total. The cost is real and it's borne by them.

**Fees** charge the people who actually transact. Visible, voluntary in the sense that you can decline to transact, and paid only by users.

Both are taxes. **The useful move is to treat this as a question of tax incidence** — who really bears the cost, as opposed to who hands over the money. That framing is standard in public economics and almost absent from writing about blockchains, which is exactly why it's worth bringing.

## Questions this page should answer

- Why are there only two sources? (Value has to come from somewhere: either dilute the existing stock or charge the flow.)
- Who actually bears the cost of issuance? Holders — but is that uniform? Consider who holds, who stakes, and who merely transacts.
- Who bears the cost of fees? Users directly — but how much is passed on to the end users of applications built on top?
- **Why might a system prefer one over the other?** Issuance charges everyone a little without them noticing; fees charge users visibly but spare holders. Those have different political economies.
- What happens as **issuance falls toward zero**? The burden shifts entirely onto users. Is that sustainable, and what does it imply about required fee levels?
- Is there a third option? (Treasury funding, inflation of a separate token, external subsidy — all tried elsewhere. Worth a sentence on why neither of the big two chains does this.)
